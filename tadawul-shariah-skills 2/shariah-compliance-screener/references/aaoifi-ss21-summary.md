# AAOIFI Shariah Standard No. 21 — Detailed Summary

## What is AAOIFI?

The **Accounting and Auditing Organization for Islamic Financial Institutions** (AAOIFI), based in Bahrain, is the global standard-setter for Islamic finance. Its standards are adopted (in whole or in part) by central banks and capital market authorities in Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, UAE, Qatar, Oman, Sudan, Syria, Jordan, Lebanon, and others.

## Standard No. 21 — "Financial Paper (Shares and Bonds)"

Issued in May 2004; revised periodically. Governs the permissibility of trading and investing in shares.

### Key Rulings Relevant to Stock Investors

**On the permissibility of shareholding in general (Section 2/1):**
> Shareholding is permissible if the company's purpose is permissible — i.e., not engaged in prohibited activities like alcohol production, swine trading, or interest-based transactions.

**On companies with mixed activities (Section 3/4):**
A company with a permissible core business but some impermissible secondary activity may be invested in IF:
1. Non-permissible activity is not the main business.
2. Impermissible income < 5% of total income.
3. Investor performs purification (donating impermissible portion).
4. Shareholder objects to impermissible activity through their voice/vote when possible.

### The Three Financial Ratios

#### 1. Debt Ratio
- **Formula:** Total interest-bearing debt ÷ 12-month average market capitalization
- **Threshold:** < 30%
- **Includes:** Conventional loans, conventional bonds issued, interest-bearing credit lines
- **Excludes:** Sukuk, Islamic financing (Murabaha, Ijarah), trade payables

#### 2. Cash and Interest-Bearing Securities Ratio
- **Formula:** (Cash + interest-bearing deposits + conventional bonds held + interest-bearing receivables) ÷ 12-month average market cap
- **Threshold:** < 30%
- **Rationale:** A company that is mostly cash held in interest accounts is functionally an interest-collection vehicle.
- **Includes:** Bank deposits earning interest, T-bills, conventional money market funds
- **Excludes:** Cash in non-interest accounts, Islamic deposits, sukuk holdings, operating cash needed for the business

#### 3. Non-Permissible Income Ratio
- **Formula:** Revenue from non-permissible sources ÷ Total revenue
- **Threshold:** < 5%
- **Includes:** Interest income on cash deposits, conventional insurance premiums (if non-core), alcohol/pork sales (if any), gambling fees
- **This is the ratio that determines purification amount.**

### Why Market Capitalization, Not Total Assets?

AAOIFI's reasoning: market cap reflects what the shareholder is actually buying — a claim on the future cash flows valued by the market. Total assets includes book values that may not reflect economic reality. This makes Saudi/AAOIFI screening **stricter in bear markets** (because market cap drops while debt stays constant).

### The 12-Month Average

To smooth out volatility, AAOIFI uses the **trailing 12-month average market capitalization**, not the spot price. This prevents a single bad day from disqualifying an otherwise compliant company.

### Practical Implication for Tadawul

Saudi Capital Market Authority and most Saudi Shariah boards (Al Rajhi, AlBilad, AlInma, Almaqasid, Jadwa) follow AAOIFI SS21 with minor variations:

- **Al Rajhi** — Standard AAOIFI methodology
- **Almaqasid** — Stricter on tobacco and certain hotel companies
- **AlInma / AlBilad** — Standard AAOIFI

This is why a stock can be on one Shariah list but not another. When boards disagree, the conservative approach is to follow the **strictest** interpretation.

### Frequency of Review

- Tadawul/Argaam list: Quarterly (after each earnings release)
- AAOIFI standard: Annual review encouraged
- Investor responsibility: Re-check before each new purchase
