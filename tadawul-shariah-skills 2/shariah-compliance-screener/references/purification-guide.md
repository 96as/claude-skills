# Dividend & Capital Gain Purification Guide

## What is Purification (Tathir / تطهير)?

Purification is the act of removing the impure portion of investment income earned from a Shariah-compliant company that nonetheless has a small percentage of non-permissible revenue (e.g., interest on cash deposits).

**It is NOT zakat.** Zakat is a separate obligation on wealth. Purification is "cleansing" — removing what is not yours to keep.

## Who Needs to Purify?

Anyone holding stock in a company that has been screened as **compliant but with non-zero non-permissible income**. If the company has 0% non-permissible income, no purification is needed.

## What to Purify

### 1. Dividends (Unanimous View)
All scholars agree: dividends from a mixed-income company must be purified.

**Formula:**
```
Purification per dividend = (Non-Permissible Income % / 100) × Dividend Received
```

**Example:**
- Saudi Telecom Company (7010) pays you SAR 2,500 in annual dividends
- STC's non-permissible income ratio is reported as 1.8% (illustrative)
- Purification = 0.018 × 2,500 = **SAR 45**

### 2. Capital Gains (Disputed)

**Two views among scholars:**

**View A (Stricter — AAOIFI majority):** Capital gains must also be purified using the same percentage.
```
Capital Gain Purification = (Non-Permissible Income % / 100) × (Sell Price − Buy Price) × Shares
```

**View B (More lenient — held by some scholars including parts of the Saudi establishment):** Capital gains do not require purification because they reflect market valuation, not the company's mixed income directly.

**Recommendation for the cautious:** Apply purification to capital gains using View A. This is the safer position.

## How to Pay Purification

### Where the money goes
- General charity (sadaqah)
- The poor and needy
- Public welfare projects (hospitals, schools, water wells)
- **NOT** mosque construction, Quran printing, or anything that earns the donor reward — purification is removing impurity, not earning thawab.
- **NOT** to your own family members or anyone you're financially responsible for.
- **NOT** as zakat (cannot double-count).

### When to pay
- Most scholars: Pay annually with a calculation review.
- Some prefer: Pay each time a dividend is received.
- Acceptable practice: Track owed purification in a ledger and pay before year-end.

## Special Cases

### What if I owned a stock that became non-compliant?

Two-step approach:
1. **Sell within a reasonable timeframe** (most scholars say 90 days; some allow until end of fiscal year).
2. Purify the **entire profit** earned during the period of non-compliance — not just the impure %.

### What if a stock pays a stock dividend / bonus shares?

Stock dividends (bonus shares) — same rule applies. Calculate purification as if the bonus shares were sold at market price on the day received, then purify the equivalent cash value.

### What about dividend reinvestment?

If you reinvest the dividend automatically, you owe purification on the gross dividend BEFORE reinvestment. Pay purification separately from cash.

### What if I lose money on a stock?

No purification on capital losses. Purification only applies to dividends received and capital gains realized. (You still owed purification on any dividends earned along the way, even if the stock ultimately lost value.)

## Tools

- Some apps (Zoya, Islamicly, Musaffa) calculate dividend purification automatically per stock.
- For Tadawul stocks, **Argaam** sometimes publishes the purification rate on the company's Shariah profile.
- For unlisted purification rates, use the company's annual interest income ÷ total revenue from the income statement.

## A Note on Intention

Purification is about **financial cleansing**, not blame. A company with 1% interest income is not "evil" — it's reflecting the reality that even good businesses operate in a world full of conventional finance. The investor's responsibility is just to remove that impure portion from their own gains. Nothing more, nothing less.
