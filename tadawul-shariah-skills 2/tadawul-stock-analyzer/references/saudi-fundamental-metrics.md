# Saudi Fundamental Metrics — What's "Good" in a Tadawul Context

Different markets have different "normal" ranges for valuation and quality metrics. Here's how to interpret them in the Saudi context.

## Valuation Metrics

### P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings)
| Range | Interpretation in Saudi context |
|---|---|
| < 10 | Cheap — but check why (cyclical lows, distress, sector out of favor) |
| 10 – 18 | Reasonable for mature sectors (banks, petrochem, telecom) |
| 18 – 30 | Premium — usually growth stocks (healthcare, tech, retail) |
| > 30 | Expensive — needs strong growth narrative to justify (e.g., new IPOs, hot themes) |

**TASI overall** typically trades at P/E 18-22.

### P/B Ratio (Price-to-Book)
| Range | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| < 1 | Trading below book — often distressed or asset-heavy |
| 1 – 2 | Mature, capital-intensive (utilities, basic industries) |
| 2 – 4 | Healthy mature business (banks often here) |
| > 4 | Premium business (high-margin, asset-light) |

### Dividend Yield
| Range | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| 0 – 2% | Growth-oriented; reinvesting all profits |
| 2 – 4% | Healthy growing dividend |
| 4 – 7% | High income — common for Saudi mature large-caps |
| > 7% | Either a value opportunity OR distress signal — investigate |

**Note:** Saudi market is notably dividend-friendly. STC, SABIC, Aramco often yield 4-7%. This is a feature, not a bug.

### EV/EBITDA
- Less commonly cited in Saudi reports, but useful for cross-border comparison
- **Saudi norms:** 6-12x for industrials; 10-15x for healthcare/consumer

## Quality Metrics

### Return on Equity (ROE)
| Range | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| < 5% | Weak — capital is not earning much |
| 5 – 12% | Average for mature/cyclical sectors |
| 12 – 20% | Good |
| > 20% | Excellent — sustainable? |

### Return on Invested Capital (ROIC)
- More important than ROE for capital-intensive Saudi names
- **> 10%** is good for petrochemicals, telecom, real estate
- **> 15%** is excellent

### Net Margin
| Sector | Healthy Net Margin |
|---|---|
| Petrochemicals | 8-15% (cyclical) |
| Telecom | 12-20% |
| Banking | 25-40% (different metric basis — net interest margin matters more) |
| Healthcare | 10-20% |
| Retail | 5-12% |
| Utilities | 8-15% |
| Mining | Highly variable with commodity cycle |

### Debt-to-Equity
- Watch this carefully if Shariah compliance matters — high debt may push ratios over 30%
- **Saudi norms:**
  - Banks: D/E is irrelevant (different framework)
  - Real Estate: 0.5-1.5 typical
  - Petrochemicals: 0.3-0.8
  - Telecom: 0.5-1.0
  - Utilities: 1.0-2.0 (capital intensive)

### Current Ratio
- Above 1.5 = healthy short-term liquidity
- Below 1.0 = warning sign for some sectors (not for banks)

## Growth Metrics

### Revenue Growth (YoY)
| Range | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Negative | Decline — investigate why (cyclical? competitive?) |
| 0-5% | Mature, possibly inflation-tracking |
| 5-15% | Healthy growth |
| 15-30% | Strong growth — usually mid-cap or theme stock |
| > 30% | Hyper-growth — often unsustainable; investigate |

### EPS Growth
- Watch for 1-time items inflating/deflating EPS
- Sustainable EPS growth = revenue growth × margin expansion
- Be skeptical of EPS growth driven mainly by buybacks (less common in Saudi)

### Earnings Stability
- Saudi market has many cyclical names (petchem, mining, banks tied to oil cycle)
- Look at 5-year earnings range, not just last year

## Saudi-Specific Context for Metrics

### 1. Government Linkage
Many Saudi blue-chips have heavy government / PIF ownership:
- **Aramco:** Majority government; PIF holdings
- **STC:** PIF majority
- **SABIC:** Aramco majority
- **Maaden:** PIF majority
- **ACWA Power:** PIF stake

This often means:
- Stable dividends (gov't wants the income)
- Less aggressive buybacks
- Capex aligned with Vision 2030 priorities
- Some downside protection (gov't unlikely to let them collapse)

### 2. Vision 2030 Catalyst Companies
Companies likely to benefit from sovereign spending themes:
- **Tourism:** Cenomi (4071), Dur Hospitality (4010), various
- **Mining:** Maaden (1211)
- **Renewables:** ACWA Power (2082)
- **Healthcare:** Mouwasat (4002), Al Habib (4013), Dallah (4004)
- **Consumer / domestic:** Almarai (2280), Jarir (4190), Extra (4003)
- **Logistics:** Saudi Ports/Logistics, SAL

### 3. Oil Price Sensitivity
- **Aramco (2222)** — Direct correlation
- **SABIC (2010), Petrochems (2350, 2380)** — Input costs and margin sensitive
- **Banks** — Indirect (oil → economy → loan demand → margins)
- **Consumer / retail** — Indirect (oil → fiscal → consumer spending)

### 4. Foreign Flows
TASI is in MSCI EM and FTSE EM indices. Index rebalancing creates flows:
- Inclusion factor changes → forced buying or selling
- Watch MSCI/FTSE quarterly review dates

### 5. Hajj/Umrah Seasonality
- Hospitality, transport, retail, telecom all see seasonal volume around Hajj season and Ramadan
- Worth knowing for timing rather than valuation

## Red Flags to Watch

1. **Aggressive accounts receivable growth >> revenue growth** — possible channel stuffing
2. **Operating cash flow consistently below net income** — earnings quality concern
3. **Heavy related-party transactions** — common in family-controlled Saudi names; not always bad but read disclosures
4. **Frequent equity raises diluting shareholders** — capital appetite signal
5. **Debt growing faster than EBITDA** — Shariah compliance risk!
6. **Dividend cuts** — usually a strong negative signal in Saudi (income-focused investor base)
7. **Auditor changes** — investigate why

## Green Flags

1. Consistent dividend track record (5+ years uninterrupted)
2. Net cash balance sheet (no debt at all — easy Shariah pass)
3. Low free float + heavy domestic institutional ownership (price stability)
4. Direct Vision 2030 contracts in pipeline
5. Family-controlled with reinvestment focus (e.g., Almarai, Jarir historically)
