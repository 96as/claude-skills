# Risk Management for Tadawul Investors

## The Three Risk Layers

1. **Per-trade risk** — How much you lose if a single trade goes wrong (covered by position sizing)
2. **Portfolio-level risk** — How concentrated your positions are; your exposure to a single sector/event
3. **Account-level risk** — Your overall capital, drawdown tolerance, runway

## Per-Trade Risk Limits

| Trader Type | Risk per Trade |
|---|---|
| Just learning | 0.25% – 0.5% |
| Building track record | 0.5% – 1% |
| Established edge | 1% – 2% |
| Professional, validated edge | 2% – 3% (rare) |

> Beginning short-term traders should **not** exceed 1% per trade until they have 50+ trades of data showing their actual win rate and average R/R.

## Portfolio-Level Limits

### Concentration Rules
- Max **15%** in any single stock
- Max **30%** in any single sector
- Max **50%** in top 5 positions

### Diversification (Shariah-Compliant Saudi Universe)
With ~250+ Shariah-compliant Tadawul stocks available, you have plenty of options. Aim for exposure across:

- Energy / Petrochemicals
- Materials / Mining / Chemicals
- Consumer Staples (food, retail)
- Healthcare
- Telecom
- Real Estate / REITs
- Utilities
- Islamic Banking (Al Rajhi, AlBilad, AlInma)
- Industrial / Construction
- Transportation / Logistics

### Correlation Awareness
Some stocks move together (e.g., petrochems all correlate with oil). 5 petrochem stocks = 1 effective position. Diversification is by EXPOSURE, not by COUNT.

## Account-Level Limits

### Max Drawdown Tolerance
Decide in advance: at what total account drawdown do you stop, reassess, or reduce size?

| Drawdown | Action |
|---|---|
| –5% | Review and confirm process |
| –10% | Reduce position sizes by half |
| –15% | Halt new positions; only manage existing |
| –20% | Stop trading; full process review |

This isn't punitive — it's a circuit breaker. Drawdowns happen; what matters is not making them worse with desperation trades.

### Position Aging
For short-term trades:
- If a swing trade isn't working within 1-2 weeks, reassess the thesis
- "Stop-loss by time" is as valid as stop-loss by price

### Cash Buffer
Keep **10-20% in cash** reserve. Reasons:
1. Opportunities (don't have to sell to buy)
2. Margin call protection (if any leverage)
3. Psychological — having cash reduces forced decisions

## Saudi-Specific Risk Considerations

### 1. Liquidity Risk
- Top 30 TASI names: very liquid, easy in/out
- Mid-caps: moderate; use limit orders
- Small/Nomu: thin; can take days to exit a position
- **Rule:** Don't take a position you can't exit in 3 trading days at most

### 2. Daily Limit Risk (±10%)
- A stock can "limit-down" — you can't sell at any price until tomorrow
- This means your stop-loss might gap through
- Mitigation: Smaller positions in higher-volatility names

### 3. Weekend Gap Risk
- Friday close → Sunday open: 2-day news exposure
- Especially relevant for: oil-linked stocks, US-correlated stocks, geopolitically sensitive names
- Mitigation: 
  - Lighter positions over the weekend if news risk is high
  - Cash position for "gap-up opportunities" (entries after weekend gap fills)

### 4. Single-Catalyst Risk
- Earnings, AGMs, dividend ex-dates can all gap a stock
- Saudi companies often pre-announce results — but surprise still happens
- Mitigation: Reduce or close positions before known events if you have no edge on the catalyst

### 5. Government / Regulatory Risk
- Saudi market is influenced by sovereign decisions (Vision 2030 contracts, fuel pricing, fiscal stance)
- Some announcements come Thursday evening/Sunday morning
- Mitigation: Stay informed via Saudi Exchange disclosures and major news outlets

### 6. Currency Risk
- Minimal for SAR-denominated investing (peg to USD at 3.75)
- Becomes relevant only if you compare to non-USD assets

### 7. Sector Concentration of TASI
- Banks (~30%) and Petrochem/Energy (~30%) dominate
- A "diversified" Saudi portfolio is still heavy on these sectors
- For real diversification, consider sector ETFs or direct positions in healthcare, telecom, consumer

## Risk Management Process — Per Trade

Before entering ANY trade, answer:

1. ☐ Is this stock Shariah-compliant (verified within last quarter)?
2. ☐ Where is my entry?
3. ☐ Where is my stop-loss (and is it logical, not arbitrary)?
4. ☐ Where is my target (target ≥ 1.5× risk away)?
5. ☐ What position size keeps risk ≤ my per-trade limit?
6. ☐ Does this position respect my concentration limits?
7. ☐ Is liquidity sufficient for my position size?
8. ☐ What's my time-stop (when do I reassess if it goes nowhere)?
9. ☐ What macro/sector context could blow up the thesis?
10. ☐ Have I journaled the rationale?

## Risk Management Process — Per Week

- [ ] Review open positions — still valid thesis?
- [ ] Check weekly performance vs benchmark (TASI / Shariah index)
- [ ] Review any closed trades — what worked, what didn't?
- [ ] Update position sizing based on current account size
- [ ] Check upcoming week's calendar (earnings, ex-dates, macro)
- [ ] Reverify Shariah status if quarter just ended

## Drawdown Recovery Math (Important Reality Check)

| Drawdown | Required Gain to Recover |
|---|---|
| –10% | +11% |
| –20% | +25% |
| –30% | +43% |
| –50% | +100% |
| –75% | +300% |

**Lesson:** It's vastly easier to **prevent** drawdowns than to recover from them. Risk management protects your compounding ability.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Review monthly. Patterns will emerge — both your wins and your mistakes.

## A Note on Speculation

You mentioned "stock speculation" — this is fine, but speculation requires MORE discipline, not less:
- Defined risk per trade (smaller, not bigger)
- Strict stops (honored, not hoped through)
- Higher win-rate or higher R/R needed to be profitable after costs
- Time-stops (don't hold "speculation" indefinitely — it stops being speculation and becomes hope)

Short-term trading also concentrates the impact of mistakes (fewer trades for randomness to average out). Be patient with sample size before judging your edge.
