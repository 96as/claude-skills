# Portfolio Management Frameworks for Tadawul Investors

## Three Main Styles

### 1. Active / Concentrated
- 5-10 positions
- High conviction
- Frequent monitoring
- Higher volatility, higher potential return
- **Best for:** Investors with time, knowledge, and emotional discipline

### 2. Diversified / Core-Satellite
- "Core" of stable blue-chips (60-70% of portfolio)
- "Satellites" of higher-conviction or thematic plays (30-40%)
- Moderate turnover
- **Best for:** Most retail investors

### 3. Passive / Index-Tracking
- Buy a Saudi Shariah-compliant ETF or replicate an index
- Minimal monitoring
- Tracks market closely
- **Best for:** Investors who don't want to pick stocks
- **Saudi options:** AlBilad MSCI Saudi Arabia Islamic Net Total Return ETF, AlJazira Mawten REIT, etc.

## Concentration Rules (Reiteration)

| Rule | Limit |
|---|---|
| Single position max | 15% |
| Single sector max | 30% |
| Top 5 positions max | 50% |
| Cash floor | 10-20% |
| Number of positions | 5-15 |

## Rebalancing Strategies

### Time-Based
- Quarterly or semi-annually
- Bring weights back to target
- Disciplined but may rebalance unnecessarily

### Threshold-Based
- Only rebalance when a position drifts > 5 percentage points from target
- More efficient (fewer trades)
- Recommended for cost-conscious investors

### Hybrid
- Threshold check at each quarterly Shariah re-screen
- Force rebalance only if needed

## Tax & Cost Awareness

For Saudi resident individuals:
- No capital gains tax → frees you to rebalance
- Trading costs ~0.3-0.4% round-trip → don't rebalance unnecessarily
- VAT 15% on commission

For non-resident foreign investors:
- May face withholding on dividends (treaty-dependent)
- Consult tax advisor

### Aggressive Short-Term Trader Style

- 4-6 active positions
- Each position has defined entry/stop/target
- Cash often 20-40% (because positions cycle in/out frequently)
- Less concern with sector "balance" — driven by setup quality
- Strict per-trade risk management (use position-sizer skill)

## Key Mental Models

### The "Why Do I Own This?" Test
For every position, you should be able to answer in one sentence:
- Why I own it
- What would make me sell
- What's my time horizon

If you can't answer these, you don't really own the position with conviction — you're just storing capital there.

### The "Can I Add Today?" Test
If you wouldn't buy more of this stock today at current price, why are you holding it?
- Not always a sell signal (taxes, costs, etc.)
- But it IS a flag to revisit the thesis

### The "Sleep Well" Test
If a single position can give you a sleepless night, it's too big.

## Performance Attribution

When reviewing performance, distinguish:
- **Stock selection alpha** — Which positions outperformed/underperformed their sector?
- **Sector allocation alpha** — Did your sector overweights help or hurt?
- **Timing** — Were entries/exits well-timed?
- **Luck** — Some of your wins might just be beta; some losses just bad market

You learn more from process review than from P/L review.

## Journaling for Portfolio Reviews

Beyond a trade journal, keep a periodic (monthly/quarterly) **portfolio journal** entry:

- Total value, % change
- Best position, worst position
- Major changes since last review
- Mistakes spotted
- What's the macro/Saudi context right now?
- Adjustments planned for next period

This is where most traders find their actual edge — pattern recognition over time.
